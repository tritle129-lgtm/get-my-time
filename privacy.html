Get My Time · Privacy policy

Last updated: 13 October 2026

Short version: Get My Time ("GMT") reads which apps you use and for how long, so it can apply the daily limits you set. This data stays on your phone. GMT has no account, no ads, no analytics and no server. The developer never receives your data.

What GMT reads

  • App usage (Android "Usage access"): which app is on screen and when apps are opened and closed. GMT uses this to count time on the apps you limit, show the 10-second pause and the Time is up screen when an app's limit is reached. When you add an app, GMT also reads your time on it in the 7 days before, to compare later.
  • The list of apps you can open, so you can pick which ones to limit.
  • Your phone's country (from the SIM card or mobile network, or else the language setting), to suggest a currency for the price of your time. You can change the currency. GMT stores only the currency you pick, not the country.

GMT does not read your contacts, messages, photos, location, screen content or anything you do inside other apps.

What GMT stores on your phone

  • The apps you limit, their daily limits, the 10-second pause setting, the price of your time and its currency. If you enter how much you earn a day, GMT only uses it to work out that price.
  • Daily totals per app: time used, cost, and how often the 10-second pause and the buttons on it and on the Time is up screen were used. These are kept until the end of the next year, then deleted.

Permissions and why

  • Usage access: to measure time on the apps you limit.
  • Display over other apps: to show the 10-second pause and the Time is up screen.
  • Notifications and a foreground service: Android requires a visible notification while GMT watches your limits in the background.
  • Run at startup: so your limits keep working after the phone restarts or GMT updates.

What leaves your phone

  • Nothing, by GMT itself. GMT does not send your usage, settings or totals anywhere.
  • Beta stats (testers only): these leave the phone only when you tap Share and pick an app, such as email or a chat app. You see the full text first. It holds your totals (minutes per day, pause and button counts), the GMT version, your phone brand and model and the Android version. It holds no app names. The app you pick sends it, under that app's own privacy policy.
  • My progress share card: an image that leaves the phone only when you tap Share and pick an app. It shows the time and money you got back, and app names only if you turn them on. The app you pick sends it, under that app's own privacy policy.
  • Send feedback: opens a Google Form in your browser. GMT puts nothing into it; only what you type and submit reaches the developer, under Google's privacy policy.
  • Updates: GMT may ask Google Play whether a newer version exists and read a small public file with the lowest version still allowed. These checks contain no data about you or your usage. As with any web request, the host of that file (GitHub) sees your IP address.
  • Android backup: if backup is on in your phone settings, Android may copy GMT's settings and totals into your own Google account backup, so they can come back on a new phone. This is managed by Google under your account; the developer cannot see it.

Deleting your data

Uninstall GMT, or go to Settings > Apps > Get My Time > Storage > Clear data. Either one deletes everything GMT stored. Removing an app from GMT keeps its past daily totals (they count toward your year) until you clear data or uninstall.

Children

GMT is made for adults and is not directed at children under 13.

Changes

If this policy changes, this page is updated and the date at the top changes. If GMT ever starts sending data off your phone, this page will say so before that version is released.
