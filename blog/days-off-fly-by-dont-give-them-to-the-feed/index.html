Days off fly by: don't give them to the feed

23 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

Topic: Daily habits

Pick one good thing for each day off before it starts. Keep the phone for people and maps, and give the feed a short window in the evening.

The days off that went by too fast

You waited all year for these days off.

The first morning, you stay in bed and check your phone. Just to see what is new.

Then it is lunch. Then it is dark.

On the last evening, you think: where did my days off go? I wanted to see my parents. I wanted to cook, walk, and wake up slowly.

The days were yours. The feed took them, one small look at a time.

This year can be different. Days off are short, so they are worth a plan.

Why rest is hard with a phone in your hand

Scrolling feels like rest. You are lying down. You are not working.

But your mind is still busy. New faces, new news, new things to want. A tired mind gets more to carry.

Real rest is quieter. A slow walk. A long meal. A nap in the afternoon. Talking with someone, with nowhere to be.

None of these need a screen. They need time, and time is what the feed takes.

Do the math for your days off

Here is an example, not a study.

Say you have 5 days off, and you spend 3 hours a day on your phone.

That is 15 hours. About one whole day, from waking up to bedtime.

Cut it to 1 hour a day and you get 10 hours back. That is a day trip, a long lunch with your family, and a good book.

Plan one good thing for each day

You do not need a big plan. One good thing a day is enough. Write them down before your days off start.

  • Day 1: Sleep in, then a slow breakfast with no phone on the table.
  • Day 2: Visit someone you have not seen in a long time.
  • Day 3: Cook a meal that takes all afternoon.
  • Day 4: Go somewhere green: a park, a beach, a hill.
  • Day 5: A lazy day. Nap, read, and do nothing on purpose.

Keep the phone for people, not the feed

Your phone still helps on days off. Use it for what you need:

  • Calls and messages with the people you love.
  • Maps, tickets and photos.
  • A short feed window in the evening, like 30 minutes, after the good thing is done.
  • Move the feed apps off your home screen for these days.
  • Leave the phone in a bag when you are with people.

A tiny check-in on the last evening

Before your days off end, ask yourself:

  • Which moment will I remember?
  • Who did I spend time with?
  • What would I keep for my next days off?
